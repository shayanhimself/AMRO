Twelve Ways to Say Genre

How a genre appears inside a movie object, whether the API publishes a genre endpoint, what that endpoint returns, and what selecting two genres at once actually means. The delimiter that means AND on TMDB means OR on Trakt. Both were checked.

Section 1

The short answer

Three of the twelve publish no genre vocabulary of their own. OMDb and Cinemeta re-export IMDb's, and IMDb has no endpoint because its vocabulary exists only as values in a TSV column.

The TV list merges what the movie list splits: 10759 Action & Adventure covers movie ids 28 and 12, 10765 Sci-Fi & Fantasy covers 878 and 14, 10768 War & Politics covers 10752. TMDB's TV vocabulary is shaped like JustWatch's. Its movie vocabulary is not.

Localisation, checked

?language=nl-NL returns 28:Actie, 12:Avontuur, 16:Animatie, 35:Komedie, 80:Misdaad, 99:Documentaire, 18:Drama, 10751:Familie. Ids stable, names translated. The rule that follows: never persist a genre name as a key. The id or slug is the key; the name is a display string that changes with locale and with the vendor's editorial mood.

Section 5

Selecting two genres: AND, or OR

Four sources accept several genres at once and they disagree about what that means. TMDB was measured directly: /3/discover/movie, total_results for each query, all four calls inside one minute.

with_genres=28|878
71,087
with_genres=28
49,673
with_genres=878
27,022
with_genres=28,878
5,615
020k40k60k80k

Genre 28 is Action, 878 is Science Fiction. Comma is AND (orange), pipe is OR. The union check holds: 49,673 + 27,022 − 5,615 = 71,080 against a measured 71,087, a drift of seven titles between calls.

The brief chose multi-select with OR semantics, applied client-side, on the grounds that a median of three genres per film makes AND return almost nothing. The measurement confirms it from the other side: TMDB's own server-side AND cuts Action and Science Fiction from 49,673 and 27,022 down to 5,615, a 92% reduction against the smaller of the two sets.

Section 6

Gotchas, collected
  • TMDB list items carry ids, detail carries objects. Two shapes for one field on one API.
  • TMDB movie and TV lists share an id space but not their contents. One cached table cannot serve both.
  • Comma means AND on TMDB and OR on Trakt. Measured on one, documented on the other.
  • Cinemeta's filter vocabulary is a subset of its item vocabulary. Items carry genres no query can select.
  • Cinemeta returns both genre and genres with identical values, legacy beside current.
  • Simkl's casing differs between body and path. Science Fiction in the object, science-fiction in the URL.
  • Simkl fails silently on an unknown genre slug: the top-level response is null, not [], and never a 404.
  • Streaming Availability genre ids are strings. They will not compare equal to TMDB integers without a cast.
  • MDBList returns ids but filters on names.
  • TheTVDB's response envelope has its own status field, unrelated to the genre payload.
  • OMDb writes "N/A", not null, when a genre is missing.
  • Trakt omits genres entirely unless you pass ?extended=full.

Section 7

What this means for AMRO

The MVP filters client-side over TMDB genre_ids, multi-select, OR semantics. Nothing here argues against either. Two things do follow.

The genre join belongs at the source boundary

Framed as "join TMDB ids to TMDB names", it looks like a repository detail. Framed as "resolve a source's genre representation into the app's own genre type", it is a boundary responsibility, and it is the same amount of code today. The difference shows the moment a second source arrives, because the shapes catalogued above are integers, strings, slugs, three-character codes, objects with three fields, and Q-items in an open ontology. Only Watchmode publishes a mapping to TMDB; every other pairing is a table someone writes and maintains.

Genre names are display strings, not keys

They are localised on TMDB, Streaming Availability and JustWatch, and they differ in case between Simkl's own body and its own URLs. A genre type keyed by an app-owned identifier, with the display name resolved for the current locale, costs nothing now and is the only version that survives a Dutch build or a second source.

The list itself is cheap

Nineteen rows, ids that have not moved, a payload measured in hundreds of bytes. Good candidate for a long-lived cache with a far lazier refresh than the 24-hour detail window, and for a bundled fallback so the filter sheet is never empty on a cold, offline start.

# TMDB: both vocabularies, and the id overlap
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMDB_TOKEN" ".../3/genre/movie/list?language=en-US"
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMDB_TOKEN" ".../3/genre/tv/list?language=en-US"

# TMDB: names localise, ids do not
curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMDB_TOKEN" ".../3/genre/movie/list?language=nl-NL"

# TMDB: comma is AND, pipe is OR
for q in 28 878 "28,878" "28%7C878"; do
  curl -s -H "Authorization: Bearer $TMDB_TOKEN" \
    "https://api.themoviedb.org/3/discover/movie?with_genres=$q" \
    | python3 -c 'import sys,json;print(json.load(sys.stdin)["total_results"])'
done
